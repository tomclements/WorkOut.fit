Create your first plan

Quick start: scroll down to the "Start a program" section and pick a preset (Shoulder-Friendly Strength, Hybrid Fat Loss, 3-Day Full Body, or Recovery Block). Each one pre-fills the whole form — just tap Create my plan.

Or build your own:

  1. Open the Planner (home page).
  2. Choose how long the plan lasts (for example 4 weeks) and which days you train.
  3. Set session length — how many minutes you have on a normal day.
  4. Pick a goal (build muscle, get stronger, endurance, or fat loss) and your experience level.
  5. Choose a split (full body, upper/lower, push/pull/legs, or bro split) and a mix (strength only, hybrid, or conditioning-focused).
  6. Pick a progression style (steady progress, wave, blocks, or keep it the same).
  7. Toggle warm-up and cool-down on or off (both on by default).
  8. Check the equipment you actually have (bodyweight alone is fine).
  9. Optional: mark injuries so those moves are avoided.
  10. Optional: check rehab / recovery areas to prefer rehab exercises targeting those joints (for example shoulder rehab includes rotator-cuff and band work).
  11. Tap Create my plan. Scroll down to see each week and day.
  12. If you don't like the exercises, tap Try different exercises (or Create again) for a new mix with the same settings.
  13. Tap Analyze to see a muscle-coverage diagram and swap individual exercises for better alternatives.
  14. When you're ready, tap Start this workout to open the runner.

You do not need an account to generate a plan. Sign in only if you want to save plans and history.

Equipment, injuries & rehab

Plan4Strength only suggests exercises that match all of the equipment you selected. If something needs a bench and you didn't check "bench," it won't appear.

Joints to protect lets you skip stressful moves for specific areas (for example shoulder protection skips overhead pressing). Each joint also has an Include rehab option that prefers gentle, targeted exercises for that area (for example rotator-cuff band work for shoulders). Rehab exercises are never restricted by the injury filter, so they always stay in the pool.

Tip: after you sign in, open Preferences to remember your default gear for next time.

Workout styles (splits)

A “split” is simply how you divide training across the week.

  • Full body — each session works most of the body. Great if you train 2–3 days a week.
  • Upper / lower — one day for upper body, one for legs. Nice balance at about 4 days a week.
  • Push / pull / legs — push days (chest, shoulders, triceps), pull days (back, biceps), leg days. Popular for muscle building when you can train often.
  • Bro split (body part) — one main body part per day (chest day, back day, and so on). Fun and focused; usually works best with 4–5 training days.

There’s no single “best” style — pick the one you’ll stick with.

Strength + HIIT in one plan

You can mix strength and HIIT in the same plan — on different days, never the same session. That matches how concurrent training is usually programmed when both muscle and conditioning matter.

  • Strength only — Every training day is lifts with normal rest (the classic Plan4Strength plan).
  • Hybrid — Most days are strength; about 1–2 days per week are full-body HIIT (timed work intervals with short rests). Best default if you want both.
  • Conditioning-focused — Most days are HIIT; 1–2 days stay strength so you keep muscle and joint resilience.

HIIT days use short work bouts (about 30 seconds) and short rest (about 10–20 seconds). Strength days keep your chosen split (full body, PPL, etc.). Your split only applies to strength days.

Why separate days? Hard intervals and heavy lifting both fatigue you. Spacing them helps quality on each day and is a practical way to get cardiorespiratory work without drowning your strength sessions.

How plans get harder (progression)

Your body improves when training is challenging and you recover. Plan4Strength can change each week automatically:

  • Steady progress (recommended) — Weeks gradually get a bit harder (more reps or sets). About every fourth week is lighter so you can recover.
  • Wave — One week emphasizes more volume (more sets), the next emphasizes harder effort. Alternating can feel fresher.
  • Blocks — A “build” phase, then a harder “push” phase, then recovery. Works especially well for longer plans (6–12 weeks).
  • Keep it the same — Difficulty stays similar week to week. Simple if you prefer not to think about periodization.

On each exercise you’ll see a short tip (for example “add a little weight if last week felt easy”). You don’t need fancy apps — just try to do a bit more over time when form is solid.

Recovery weeks are intentional. They are not “slacking.” Lighter weeks help you come back stronger and reduce burnout.

Running a workout

The runner is timer-based — no tapping for each rep. Each set is a work countdown (for example 30 seconds — aim for the listed reps), then a rest countdown, then the next set.

  1. Open Run (or Start workout from a plan).
  2. Pick the workout day from the dropdown. A preview card shows the exercises, estimated time, and focus.
  3. Configure Sound & music: choose a background style (Drive / Focus / Power / Calm / My music), and toggle Countdown tones (beeps, on by default) and Voice cues (spoken 3-2-1, off by default). Tap Test sound & beeps to preview.
  4. Tap Analyze on the preview card to see a muscle-coverage body diagram and swap individual exercises for better alternatives.
  5. Before Start, weighted moves (barbell, dumbbells, kettlebell, machines, and similar) show an optional working weight field. Bodyweight, bands, and timed warm-up / cool-down moves do not. Leave it blank if you do not know yet — blank is not saved as zero. Weights follow your kg/lb preference and are stored with the session.
  6. Tap Start workout. Work when the green timer runs; rest when the blue timer runs.
  7. Timers advance automatically. Tap Finish set early or Skip rest if you need to.
  8. Tap the ⋯ (three dots) for session options: change music, adjust volume, toggle tones/voice, skip exercise, go to previous exercise, or view all moves.
  9. At the end, if you're signed in, the session is saved to History automatically.
  • Fullscreen and screen wake lock help in the gym.
  • Dark toggles dark / high-contrast mode (better outdoors and at night).
  • TV opens cast tips and a big-timer TV mode for AirPlay / screen mirroring.
  • Before each new move you'll see an in-app demo (animated WebP or photo flip + video search). Rest screens also preview the next exercise.
  • If you leave mid-workout, you can usually Resume from where you left off.

Keyboard shortcuts

While the runner is open:

  • Space or Enter — finish the current set early (work) or skip rest.
  • Escape — close the overflow modal if it's open.

TV, AirPlay & casting

Web apps can’t push the whole UI through AirPlay the way a video app can. Use screen mirroring (or Cast tab) with the runner’s TV mode for a large timer.

  1. Open the runner → tap TV → turn on TV mode.
  2. iPhone/iPad/Mac: Control Center → Screen Mirroring → Apple TV (or AirPlay display).
  3. Chrome: menu → Cast → Cast tab → Chromecast / Google TV.
  4. Keep the phone unmuted so voice countdowns and beeps still play locally.

Account, history & favorites

  • Sign in / register — saves plans and completed workouts to your account.
  • History — past sessions with totals, a streak counter, volume and minutes charts, and per-exercise breakdowns.
  • Body weight — log daily weigh-ins (kg or lb) from the dashboard. Includes a 30-day sparkline chart and change indicator.
  • Like / dislike (👍 👎) — rank exercises on a plan or in the exercise picker. Plans prefer likes and skip dislikes when possible.
  • Preferences — default equipment, workout music, and music volume. Your last plan choices (level, goal, split, days, etc.) are remembered automatically when you create a plan.
  • Privacy — see the privacy policy for what we store, cookies, and how to request deletion.

Common questions

I’m a complete beginner. Where do I start?
Full body, 2–3 days, 20–30 minutes, beginner level, steady progress. Keep weights light until movements feel smooth.
Can I change a plan after it's generated?
Yes. Tap Analyze to swap individual exercises for compatible alternatives. You can also add exercises with the + Add exercise button, remove exercises, or turn days into rest (and vice versa).
Why is a recovery week lighter?
Your body adapts during rest as well as during hard training. Planned easy weeks help long-term progress.
Do I need special equipment?
No. Bodyweight-only plans work. More equipment just unlocks more exercise variety.
What's the difference between injuries and rehab?
Protect removes exercises that stress that area. Include rehab does the opposite — it prefers exercises that target the rehab path for that area (for example rotator-cuff band work for shoulders). You can use both together: Protect avoids harmful moves, and rehab adds therapeutic ones.
How do I swap an exercise during a workout?
Tap the ⋯ (three dots) button during any set, then tap the exercise you want to swap. You'll see a list of compatible alternatives filtered by the same slot, equipment, and injury settings. Tap one to swap it in.
Is this medical advice?
No. Plan4Strength is a planning tool. If you have health conditions or pain, check with a qualified professional before starting.

Still stuck? Create a short plan and press Start — the app is designed to be learned by using it.
